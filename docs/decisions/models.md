# Models decisions (M-)

Decisions of the models stream: hand-made blockouts in `src/shared/Defs/Blockouts/<id>.luau` and everything under `assets/models/`. Progress is in [`../progress/models.md`](../progress/models.md). The blockout format and the rig are the client stream's contract ([`../contracts/rig.md`](../contracts/rig.md), decisions C-4, C-6, C-8); this stream follows it and doesn't change it.

Each entry gives the date, the decision, the alternatives and the reason. Overrule any of them and the blockouts, the guide and this file are updated together.

### M-1 — Review in "FTGG Art" (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** each batch is built in the "FTGG Art" place from the Rojo-synced blockout modules with the client's own `BlockoutBuilder`, into three workspace folders: `BlockoutReview` (team colors, three-quarter heading), `BlockoutProfile` (facing screen-left) and `BlockoutSilhouette` (all black). Order on the near row, Chaos behind, one column per role, 18 studs apart. Screenshots use the game camera's pitch (55°, `src/client/Camera.luau`) from the south, at review distance (about 50 studs) and gameplay distance (about 100 studs). The folders are place content for review only; the modules in the repo are the source.

**Alternatives:** headless renders (`lune run render_scene`, top-down only); building models by hand in the place (not reproducible).

**Why:** the same builder the game uses, the game's camera angle, and a silhouette pass for guide 20.1's "silhouette first".

### M-2 — Faction shape language (2026-10-21, superseded by M-6)

Rejected in review the same day: Chaos read as post-apocalyptic or steampunk (exposed engines, stacks, crates, pistons, rust, a round dish) and Order's bright blue turrets looked like toys. The batch 1 blockouts other than Lancer and Ravager still use it until a direction from M-6 is picked.

**Decision:**

- **Order:** symmetric; sloped wedges for glacis, noses and turret fronts; glass cabs, optics and array faces in blue glass; long, thin guns; six small wheels on carriers. Team color on the turret or the top-facing piece.
- **Chaos:** flat-faced heavy boxes; rams and plows in front; a skirt on one side only; cabs, weapons and masts off-center; exposed engines, generators, crates, pistons and stacks; orange glass in slits and glow points; big wheels, fat short guns. Team color on the turret, rack, arm or dish.

Both factions share each role's key silhouette cue, so a role reads the same either side: scout = smallest, wheeled, tiny gun; light tank = turret and gun; artillery = longest or fattest gun; IR SAM = launcher raised on a pedestal; radar = big panel or dish above the hull; worker = crane or claw arm rising forward.

**Alternatives:** palette-only difference (C-6's palettes alone).

**Why:** guide 2.1 and 20.3 ("clean slopes vs heavy blocks") so the factions read apart even at low detail, and guide 20.1's silhouette rule.

### M-3 — IR SAM rig (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** `ord_halo` and `chs_hornet` list the pieces `turret` and `launcher`, but their weapon turns `launcher` (`turretPiece = "launcher"`, no `pitchPiece`). The blockouts make `turret` a fixed pedestal on the hull and `launcher` (pods, sensor, missiles) a child of it, pivoting on the pedestal's vertical axis. The pods are level at rest (rig.md 2). If the defs later turn `turret` and pitch `launcher` (request 1 in the progress file), the same blockouts work: the launcher's pivot is on the pedestal's axis at trunnion height.

**Alternatives:** `launcher` under `base` and `turret` as a decoration-like piece; pods modeled pre-elevated (would add a fixed offset to any future pitch).

**Why:** works with today's defs and with the rig a SAM would normally have, with no change to either.

### M-4 — Artillery at rest (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** Verdict and Hellmouth have level barrels at rest, like every pitch piece (rig.md 2: `Transform = identity` is the rest pose). Verdict reads by a 9-stud gun reaching 5 studs past the nose; Hellmouth by a fat 1.3-stud tube with a breech ring and a lip, a recoil spade and ammo crates.

**Alternatives:** model the tube elevated inside the piece (breaks the rest-pose rule and the server's pitch).

**Why:** the client's piece script pitches the barrel from the server's aim; a built-in offset would show the wrong angle.

### M-5 — Wedge orientation (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** an unrotated wedge's slope faces forward (−Z) and up, its tall face at the back; checked in a Studio side view (Verdict's glacis and turret front). Glacis plates, noses, windshields and turret fronts use no `rot`.

**Why:** settles the convention for every later blockout. Note: `chs_juggernaut`'s glacis has `rot = { 0, 180, 0 }`, which turns its tall face forward; to check in the T2 batch.

### M-6 — Visual direction: modern armies (2026-10-21)

**Decision** (the user's, after batch 1): Order is inspired by Western modern armies (Leopard 2, Abrams, Patriot): clean, angular wedge armor, flat-panel sensors, light grey or sand. Chaos is inspired by Eastern modern armies (T-90M, T-14 Armata, Pantsir): heavier, blockier, reactive-armor tiles and slat cages, more weapons per vehicle, dark olive or gunmetal; no exposed engines, stacks, crates or rust. Both: long, low, wide hulls; flat turrets toward the rear; long guns; side skirts with road wheels; sights and sensor boxes; canister launchers for SAMs; flat phased-array radars, never dishes; team color only on panels and stripes. Silhouette first, secondary shapes second, small details only if cheap. Guide 2.1 and 20.3 are updated; details in [`../art/style-guide.md`](../art/style-guide.md).

**Alternatives:** M-2 (rejected).

**Why:** a modern-warfare game; the factions should look like plausible modern armies, told apart by their real-world families and base colors.

### M-7 — Style candidates outside the Registry (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** variants for a style review go in `src/shared/Defs/Blockouts/Review/<id>_<letter>.luau`, each a valid Blockout for its def (same `id`), listed with a proposed palette in `Review/Variants.luau`. They aren't in `Blockouts/Registry`, so the game and `Blockouts.spec` keep using the registered blockouts. The review script builds them with the client's `BlockoutBuilder` and recolors each part from its palette key. Once a variant is picked it replaces the registered file, and its palette replaces the faction's entry in `Blockout.PALETTES` (which every unit uses, including the generic blockouts).

**Alternatives:** changing `Blockout.PALETTES` now (would restyle all units before a direction is picked); variants in `assets/models/` (not synced into Studio).

**Why:** compare candidates in the real builder without changing anything the game draws.

### M-8 — Team color on panels and stripes (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** team-colored parts are thin panels only: a stripe on each skirt, a panel on each turret side and a roof panel on the turret (like an air-recognition panel). Never a whole turret, hull or launcher.

**Why:** M-6 (whole blue turrets looked like toys); the roof panel keeps the team readable from the RTS camera, which mostly sees roofs.

### M-9 — Near-future look, faction roles, team color on top (2026-10-21)

**Decision** (the user's): near-future military, realistic-leaning: real modern vehicles with restrained sci-fi accents (angular armor panels, sensor strips, active protection, rail-gun details); *World in Conflict* as a mood reference only. Order plays heavy defense and strategy: sleek, wedge-shaped, high-tech, fortress-like, sand. Chaos plays the strength of armies: rugged and aggressive, rounder or domed turrets, visible reactive-armor blocks, rear slat cages, shorter and wider. Team color goes on top surfaces (turret roof, hull top), since the camera looks down at 35°–80°. Phase 2 (meshes) starts now; the part-built blockouts stay as gameplay placeholders and aren't polished further. Guide 2.1 and [`../art/style-guide.md`](../art/style-guide.md) are updated.

**Why:** the camera mostly sees roofs; the faction roles give each look a purpose.

### M-10 — Chaos base color: gunmetal (2026-10-21, recommended)

**Decision:** gunmetal (76, 80, 84) over olive (80, 86, 60), from a test on the Coastline terrain in "FTGG Art" (pitch 35°, 55°, 80°; 80, 220, 450 and 900 studs): olive disappears into the grass, which covers most of the map; gunmetal reads on grass and sand and apart from Order's sand.

**Alternatives:** olive (realistic camouflage, poor readability in an RTS).

### M-11 — Mesh pipeline (2026-10-21, approved)

**Decision:** units are modeled by Python scripts run in headless Blender 5.2 (no AI base mesh for vehicles), baked to one 1024 PBR texture set per unit, exported as FBX, imported with Studio's 3D Importer (or the Open Cloud Assets API with a key) and rigged in Studio by a script. Team color through the color map's alpha in `AlphaMode.Overlay`. Steps and the generator license survey: [`../art/pipeline.md`](../art/pipeline.md).

**Alternatives:** AI-generated base meshes (license limits and cleanup cost, see pipeline.md); separate team-colored parts (more draw calls and pieces).

**Why:** reproducible, reviewable as diffs, exact to the rig and the budget.

### M-12 — Mesh budgets (2026-10-21)

**Decision** (the user's): the Lancer is built at up to about 4,000 triangles with Roblox's automatic level of detail (`RenderFidelity.Automatic`), not guide 20.1's 1,500, which was meant for simple low-poly art. A 100-unit frame-time test decides whether the higher budget holds; per-class budgets (hero units, line units, structures) are proposed from it in [`../art/pipeline.md`](../art/pipeline.md). The Lancer came out at 3,016.

### M-13 — Import fixes in the rig script (2026-10-21)

**Decision:** Studio's 3D Importer reads the Blender FBX as centimeters (x100) and turns it 180° about Y. `rig_model.luau`'s `normalize(src, rig, 0.01, true)` undoes both, `check` compares every piece with `rig.json`, and `rig` clears the pivot offsets that "Set Pivot to Scene Origin" leaves. Importer defaults stay as they are.

**Alternatives:** changing the FBX export (global scale, forward axis) or the importer's File Transform settings: each needs another import to verify, and a wrong guess costs a re-upload.

**Why:** one fixed, checked correction is cheaper than tuning two tools' axis conventions; `check` catches it if either changes.

### M-14 — Heavy Order, light Chaos (2026-10-22)

**Decision** (the user's, following A's `docs/design/factions.md`): Order is the heavy side (tougher, slower, more expensive), Chaos the light side (faster, cheaper, built for numbers). The looks of M-9 stay (Order sleek and wedge-shaped, Chaos rugged), but the armor reads the gameplay: Order with thick plates, add-on armor modules, deep skirts, a low and broad stance; Chaos with thin plates, armor as separate blocks and cages, partial skirts showing the running gear, a compact hull higher off the ground. This replaces M-9's "Chaos shorter and wider" with "shorter and narrower". Cues per feature: [`../art/style-guide.md`](../art/style-guide.md), "Reading heavy vs light".

**Consequence:** the Lancer mesh (review 2) reads mid-weight: thin skirt plates and visible road wheels. To match, its next revision gets thicker plate edges, deeper skirts and turret add-on armor.

### M-15 — BAR model style (2026-10-22)

**Decision** (the user's): units are rendered in Beyond All Reason's model style on our near-future shapes: strong, slightly exaggerated silhouettes (turret and gun about 15–25 % bigger than real), clean hard-surface painted metal with crisp panel lines and edge highlights baked in, no camouflage, large bold team-color plates designed into the model (turret roof, hull top, side plates), small emissive accents (Order cyan, Chaos orange). BAR screenshots in `assets/reference/bar/` are style references only; BAR's model files and textures are never downloaded or copied. Replaces M-9's camouflage and textured realism; the faction shapes (M-9, M-14) stay. Style guide and guide 2.1 updated.

**Emissive in the pipeline:** the 3D Importer uploads only maps a material uses, so the build exports a tiny `emissive_probe` quad whose color map is the emissive mask; the rig step reads its asset ID into each SurfaceAppearance's `EmissiveMaskContent` (tint and strength from `rig.json`) and deletes the quad.

### M-16 — Blockout palettes follow the style guide (2026-10-22)

**Decision:** `Blockout.PALETTES` uses the style guide's paints: Order hull sand-grey (178, 174, 160) and cyan glass (90, 210, 255); Chaos hull gunmetal (76, 80, 84), steel-grey metal instead of rust, orange glass (255, 140, 50). Every part-built blockout and the generic ones change color with it; shapes don't change.

**Why:** the old Chaos palette (rust, orange glow on dark iron) is what M-6 and M-15 rule out; new structure blockouts should read in the new faction colors.

### M-17 — Pending blockouts (2026-10-22)

**Decision:** blockouts for units that exist only in a design proposal (A's `docs/design/factions.md`: Warden, Minotaur, Locust, Cyclops, Barrier, Citadel) go in `src/shared/Defs/Blockouts/Pending/`, with `Pending/Registry.luau` listing the def fields they assume (id, faction, tier, domain, footprint, pieces) and review poses (the Warden's packed and deployed). They're validated against those stub defs but not registered, so the game and `Blockouts.spec` don't see them. When the sim session adds a def with the same pieces, the file moves up and gets registered. Request to the sim session in the progress file.

**Review poses in Edit mode:** Studio doesn't apply `Motor6D.Transform` in Edit mode, so the review script places posed pieces directly on review copies.

**Update (2026-10-22, S-64):** the sim session added defs for the Warden (`ord_warden`, footprint 1, pieces base / spades / turret / barrel / muzzle) and the Minotaur (`chs_minotaur`, footprint 2, pieces base / ram / turret / barrel / muzzle; `ram` is the demolition charge's turret piece and muzzle). Both blockouts moved up and are registered: the Warden was rebuilt at footprint 1 with one hinged `spades` plate (no shield), the Minotaur's ram plate became the jointed `ram` piece. M-3's SAM rig now matches the defs too (S-64: turn `turret`, pitch `launcher`).

### M-18 — BAR tanks approved; review fixes (2026-10-22)

**Decision:** the user approved the BAR style (M-15) for both tanks and retired the camouflage Lancers (review 2a/2b: their files stay, the Studio copies under `ModelReview` are removed). Fixes from the review:
- **Lancer paint:** it rendered near-white with a blue tint in Studio. Measured in the same shot, the mesh's hull top drew (180, 188, 187) against the blockout's (170, 177, 169): sky reflections on fairly glossy paint plus a near-white edge highlight. The paint is now rougher (0.62), a touch warmer and darker (174, 168, 150), with a sand edge highlight (214, 210, 196), darker seams and more ambient occlusion. Target: the mesh draws like the M-16 blockout.
- **Ravager:** gunmetal lifted to (86, 90, 95) with a brighter edge highlight, so it doesn't sink into its own shadow at 35°. The whole unit is scaled (0.985, 0.9, 0.93) after building (`lib.Piece.scale`, pivots included): 5.67 × 3.35 studs against the Lancer's 6.3 × 3.70, so 10% narrower and lower (Chaos reads lighter, M-14). Orange emissive unchanged (small).
- **Team color:** applied at runtime, never baked. B's `ModelPool` sets `BasePart.Color` on `TeamColor`-tagged parts; on a meshed piece the color map's alpha (Overlay) masks it to the panels, and the emissive properties are independent. Written into `docs/contracts/rig.md` section 6 and checked with four team colors.

**Why:** the user's review of the overnight batch (2026-10-22).

### M-19 — Blockout review fixes (2026-10-22)

**Decision:**
- **Warden** (packed vs deployed must read at 220 studs): client-only pieces `jacks` (four legs, pads to the ground when deployed), `shieldL` / `shieldR` (front shield wings hinged at the hull's front corners: along the sides when packed, closed into a wall wider than the hull with a gun slot when deployed), a bigger `spades` plate (5.8 × 3.2) whose front face carries hazard stripes: hidden behind the turret when packed, face up on the ground when deployed. The deployed review pose also lowers the hull 0.4 and raises the gun 12°. The client's piece script still needs to animate the new pieces (progress, request 6).
- **Minotaur vs Cyclops** (they read alike as black shapes): the Minotaur is now low (turret top 3.4), wide and front-heavy, with an 11.8-stud ram plate wider than its tracks. The Cyclops is tall: a 7-stud mortar tube at 75° and the big eye on a 4.6-stud mast (top at 10.5). The tube is fixed to the turret: the weapon pitches −5° to 60° from a level rest (rig.md 2), and a tube authored near-vertical would point backwards when aiming. So the `barrel` piece is the short elevating cradle at the tube's foot, and the muzzle sits at the tube's mouth on the turret (a mortar ranges by charge).
- **Reckoning** (must never read as a Citadel from above): two split blast doors (6 × 12 each) fill most of the roof, ringed by a yellow and black hazard edge. `hatch` is the left door; the right door is the client-only `hatch2`.
- **Palette:** new key `hazard` (228, 184, 40), the same in every faction (rig.md 4). The Chaos `hull` moved to (86, 90, 95) with the Ravager's paint, so mesh and blockout match.

**Why:** the user's review of the overnight batch (2026-10-22).

### M-20 — T1 vs T2, and no generic blockouts left (2026-10-22)

**Decision:** per role, the T2 version must read as the bigger or more elaborate upgrade at 55° and 220 studs. Where the footprint can't grow, it gets one signature piece: Order advanced airfield a big radome, Chaos advanced airfield a team-colored ski-jump ramp; Order advanced shipyard a covered building dock, Chaos advanced shipyard a giant hammerhead crane; Bastion and Maw stand on raised plinths with a sensor or ammo drum; both advanced extractors widen with side tanks. Every unit def now has a hand-made blockout (133); the aircraft and ships come from two small generator scripts (`assets/models/tools/blockouts/gen_air.py`, `gen_naval.py`) that write plain Luau data files, so the files read like the hand-written ones and can be edited by hand after.

**Why:** the user's queue items 8 and 9 (2026-10-22).

### M-21 — Effect assets (2026-10-22)

**Decision:** effect assets (the Order shield first) are white or greyscale with alpha and are tinted by the client with the team color at runtime (`BasePart.Color`, `Beam.Color`, `ParticleEmitter.Color`, `Decal.Color3`). The models stream makes them (`assets/fx/<effect>/build.py`, headless Blender, procedural textures, so everything is original) and documents sizes, pivots and the property to tint in `docs/contracts/fx.md`; the client stream builds and animates the effect. An emitter device's emissive accent stays the faction color (M-16).

**Why:** the user's shield request (2026-10-22).
